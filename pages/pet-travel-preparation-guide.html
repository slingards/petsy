Pet Travel Preparation: A Checklist Before a Trip

Travel becomes easier when the animal's normal needs are planned before the journey rather than improvised on the day.

Whether a trip is short or long, preparation should begin with the species, destination and transport method. Requirements can vary substantially between jurisdictions and carriers.

Confirm the rules first

Check the current requirements of the destination, transport provider and any jurisdictions crossed during the trip. Documentation, vaccinations, health certificates, identification and import rules may apply depending on the animal and route.

Use secure identification

Make sure identification details are current. Carry copies of relevant records and keep emergency contact information accessible rather than relying on a single phone image.

Choose an appropriate carrier or enclosure

The animal should have enough room to maintain a comfortable posture while remaining securely contained. Introduce the carrier before travel so it does not become an unfamiliar object associated only with a stressful journey.

Plan food and water

Keep the usual diet as consistent as practical. Bring enough of the normal food for delays, and plan access to water according to the species and transport conditions.

Build in breaks when appropriate

For road travel, plan safe stops for species that need them. Never leave an animal unattended in unsafe temperatures. For air or other transport, follow the provider's exact instructions.

Reduce unnecessary stress

Keep the travel environment quiet, stable and secure. Familiar bedding or another safe familiar item can help some animals. Avoid giving sedatives unless specifically directed by a veterinarian.

Before departure: Save the phone number of a veterinarian at the destination and know where emergency veterinary care is available.
